Research Overview

My training is in physical and computational chemistry, with experience using density functional theory, molecular dynamics, statistical mechanical models, and molecular simulation workflows to study chemical systems. My current interests extend from computational chemistry and molecular modeling into cheminformatics, machine learning, scientific computing education, and undergraduate research.

At Trine University, I am especially interested in research directions that allow students to connect chemical concepts with computational tools. Some projects are chemistry-heavy, while others sit at the overlap of chemistry, biology, coding, and data science.

Research Themes

Computational Chemistry and Molecular Modeling

Computational chemistry allows us to study chemical systems at a molecular level using models grounded in quantum mechanics, statistical mechanics, and molecular simulation. My work uses these tools to investigate molecular structure, energetics, reactivity, and structure-property relationships.

This area is a good fit for students interested in physical chemistry, quantum chemistry, catalysis, materials chemistry, or molecular modeling.

Cheminformatics and Molecular Property Prediction

Cheminformatics uses molecular structures, descriptors, and datasets to identify patterns in chemical behavior. I am especially interested in interpretable models that connect molecular structure to experimentally observed properties.

One developing direction in my group is molecular taste prediction, where students can combine chemical structure data, RDKit descriptors, functional group analysis, and low-cost quantum chemical descriptors to study relationships between molecular structure and taste labels such as sweet, bitter, sour, salty, and umami.

Scientific Computing and Data-Driven Chemistry

Modern chemistry increasingly depends on the ability to work with data, write reproducible code, and communicate results clearly. My group uses Python-based workflows for molecular data analysis, visualization, cheminformatics, and computational chemistry.

This theme is especially appropriate for students who want to build transferable skills in coding, data analysis, scientific communication, and research computing.

Undergraduate Research in Chemistry, Biology, and Coding

A major goal of my research program is to create projects that are meaningful, publishable when possible, and appropriate for undergraduate researchers. Some projects are chemistry-heavy, while others sit closer to biology, cheminformatics, or data science.

Students can enter the group from different starting points. Some may begin by learning Python and analyzing molecular datasets. Others may work on computational chemistry calculations, chemical structure classification, molecular visualization, or literature-based project development.

Current and Developing Projects

Molecular Taste Prediction

Developing interpretable cheminformatics and machine learning workflows to study relationships between molecular structure and taste labels.

Molecular Modeling of Catalytic Systems

Using computational chemistry to study structure, energetics, and reactivity in catalytic and materials systems.

Scientific Computing Education

Building teaching and research materials that help students learn coding, data analysis, modeling, and reproducible scientific workflows.

Student Research Opportunities

Students do not need to arrive with all of the technical skills already in place. Many projects in my group are designed so that students can build skills over time while contributing to a real research question.

Students working with me may learn to use Python for data analysis, build molecular datasets, analyze chemical structures, run computational chemistry calculations, visualize molecular properties, or explore machine learning models for chemical prediction. Depending on the project, students may also gain experience with scientific writing, presentations, GitHub, LaTeX, and high-performance or high-throughput computing workflows.

Good fits for this research group include students who are curious, careful, willing to learn new tools, and interested in the overlap between chemistry, computation, biology, and data science. Prior programming experience is helpful, but it is not required for all projects.

Tools and Methods

Depending on the project, students may work with a mixture of chemical theory, molecular modeling software, programming tools, and data analysis workflows.

  • Computational chemistry: density functional theory, molecular dynamics, molecular modeling, thermochemistry, and free energy models
  • Scientific computing: Python, NumPy, data analysis, visualization, Git, and reproducible workflows
  • Cheminformatics: RDKit, molecular descriptors, functional group analysis, and molecular datasets
  • Research computing: high-performance computing, high-throughput computing, and automated workflows
  • Software: Quantum ESPRESSO, ORCA, ASE, VASP, Turbomole, Git, and LaTeX
