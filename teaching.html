Teaching

My teaching focuses on helping students connect chemical concepts with quantitative reasoning, laboratory evidence, scientific computing, and real-world problem solving.

I teach undergraduate courses in general chemistry, physical chemistry, laboratory chemistry, and data science in chemistry and biology. Across these courses, I try to make technical material approachable while still giving students practice with the kinds of thinking used by working scientists.

Teaching Philosophy

Chemistry asks students to move between several ways of thinking: atoms and molecules, equations and models, experimental data, graphs, and macroscopic observations. My goal as an instructor is to help students connect these ideas rather than treating them as isolated facts or procedures.

I want students to become more confident problem solvers. That means helping them practice the habits that scientists use: asking good questions, interpreting evidence, checking assumptions, using models carefully, and explaining their reasoning clearly.

I also believe that computation and data analysis should be part of modern chemistry education. Students do not need to become expert programmers to benefit from these tools. Even basic experience with spreadsheets, Python, graphing, molecular visualization, and reproducible workflows can help students see chemistry as a quantitative and creative discipline.

In my courses and mentoring, I try to create a supportive environment where students can take intellectual risks, ask questions, revise their thinking, and build technical skills over time.

Trine University

General Chemistry I covers the fundamentals of chemistry with emphasis on atomic structure, stoichiometry, thermochemistry, properties of solutions, and properties of matter.

In my version of the course, I emphasize molecular-level reasoning, quantitative problem solving, and helping students connect chemical models to observable chemical behavior.

The General Chemistry I laboratory is quantitative in nature and supports student learning in atomic structure, stoichiometry, thermochemistry, solution behavior, and properties of matter.

In the laboratory, I want students to see data collection, uncertainty, calculations, graphing, and written explanation as central parts of doing chemistry.

General Chemistry II continues from General Chemistry I, with emphasis on chemical equilibria, thermodynamics, kinetics, acid-base reactions, electrochemistry, and properties of solutions.

I use this course to help students strengthen their ability to connect mathematical relationships with chemical interpretation, especially in equilibrium, thermodynamics, and reaction behavior.

General Chemistry II Laboratory supports topics such as chemical equilibrium, thermodynamics, kinetics, acid-base chemistry, electrochemistry, and properties of solutions through experimental work.

I emphasize quantitative data analysis, computer skills, graphing, and clearer connections between experimental measurements and chemical reasoning.

This course introduces students to data science methods applied to chemistry and biology. Students learn how molecules and biomolecules can be represented numerically, how to prepare and analyze chemical and biological datasets, and how to apply machine learning models to predict molecular and biological properties.

Students complete a final project applying data science methods to a dataset of their choice. The course is cross-listed as BIO 352.

Physical Chemistry I is an in-depth study of real gases, thermodynamics, kinetics, chemical and physical equilibrium, and electrochemistry.

In this course, I emphasize the connection between mathematical models, chemical intuition, and measurable physical behavior.

Physical Chemistry I Laboratory contains experiments connected to real gases, thermodynamics, kinetics, chemical and physical equilibrium, and electrochemistry.

The laboratory gives students practice connecting experimental measurements with physical models, uncertainty, data analysis, and scientific reporting.

Physical Chemistry II focuses on the fundamentals of quantum theory of atoms and molecules, as well as spectroscopy.

I use this course to help students build connections between mathematical models, molecular structure, spectroscopy, and chemical behavior.

Physical Chemistry II Laboratory contains experiments connected to quantum theory, molecular structure, and spectroscopy.

The laboratory emphasizes experimental design, data analysis, molecular interpretation, and clear scientific communication.

Mentoring and Student Support

I mentor undergraduate students in computational chemistry, cheminformatics, bioinformatics, Python programming, and research computing workflows. I also advise students on academic planning, course selection, and career preparation.

My mentoring style emphasizes growth over prior experience. Students do not need to already know everything before starting research or asking questions. What matters most is curiosity, consistency, careful work, and a willingness to learn.

  • Undergraduate research mentoring
  • Academic advising and course planning
  • Scientific writing and presentation support
  • Python, LaTeX, Git, and computational workflow support
  • Career preparation for chemistry, biology, data science, and graduate school pathways
